## 12 — Returns

### The core rule

> **[IMPORTANT]** **Medicine is generally not returnable.** Not on public, not on wholesale.
>
> **Only paikari buyers** get a return option, under strict conditions.

### Paikari conditions

These rules are **also printed on the memo**:

- **Bring the memo to return**
- **Within 2–3 days of sale** (counted from receipt), otherwise no return

> **[IMPORTANT]** **Exchange only** — replaced with another medicine, full value credited.
>
> If someone wants **cash**, they get **only 50%**.
>
> The memo says — "Taking cash back: 50% applies, because we only offer exchange."

**Sealed or not** — no strict rule. Staff decide case by case.

### How the return section works

#### For paikari buyers

1. In the section they see **all products they bought**
2. Those bought **within 2–3 days** show **"Returnable"**
3. Older ones have **no return option at all**
4. They can **search by date** to see memos and purchases

#### For public buyers

1. They **apply** first
2. If I approve, **they are called**
3. **Must be logged in** to return

> **[NOTE]** Someone who bought as a guest must open an account to return.

### Reason is mandatory

Both paikari and public must give a **note or reason** for the return. **Voice note** allowed too.

### Partial returns

> **[IMPORTANT]** Someone bought 1 box with 100 tablets and has sold some.
>
> So there is an **option to say how much is being returned** — 1 strip, or however much. The rate is calculated accordingly.

<!-- -->

> **[EXAMPLE]** One box costs 500 taka, 50 pieces per box → 10 taka per piece.
>
> Returning 30 pieces → **300 taka**.
>
> Calculated **at the price they paid**.

### Approval and credit

1. **Paikari buyers** return at the shop. I **check it myself and only then approve**.
2. **Outside customers** apply. I **contact them, check everything**, then decide.
3. Once approved, their account shows **"Credit: X taka"**
4. On their next purchase **that amount is deducted from the memo**, with the note **"Return product"**

### How long the credit lasts

- **Automatically deducted** on the next purchase
- If they don't buy for a long time, a **warning after 1–2 months**

> **[NOTE]** Credit can be used **only in the sector where it was returned**. Otherwise accounts get mixed up (Chapter 24).

### Where a return goes back

> **[IMPORTANT]** It's verified against the invoice and **goes back into the same lot it came from** — at that price. This keeps profit correct.
>
> Returned goods go back to stock and **are sold again**.

<!-- -->

> **[PROBLEM]** The lot it came from has run out.

<!-- -->

> **[SOLUTION]** The group reopens with just the returned goods. The purchase price hasn't changed.

### Retail refunds

> **[IMPORTANT]** **Retail customers have no profile or account.** 300–400 retail customers a day — profiles for each are impossible.
>
> So their refunds happen **only in PharmaTrack**, handled manually.

#### Finding an old sale

Three kinds of search (for both paikari and retail):

- **By memo number** — every memo has a number
- **By date**
- **By medicine name** — every sale of that medicine in recent days

> **[NOTE]** The third is especially useful — even if the memo is lost, knowing the medicine and rough time finds it.

<!-- -->

> **[PROBLEM]** The invoice can't be found at all.

<!-- -->

> **[SOLUTION]** **"Return without invoice"** — admin enters the price by hand, with a separate mark. Rare, but needed.

#### Credit for retail

No account, so no credit storage. Refunds or exchanges are **handled by hand**.

> **[NOTE]** But there is an **optional field** — "Will receive money for returned goods". Useful in rare cases. (A credit note option exists but is almost never used.)

#### Rules printed on retail memos too

> **[IMPORTANT]** "Medicine returns apply within 3 days of purchase. Not after that. Taking cash back: 50% applies, or another product is given in exchange."
>
> I set the number of days in settings.

### Returns on offer products

- **Credited** to the account, deducted on the next memo
- Taking another product → **exchange**
- Wanting cash → **50% of the offer rate**

> **[EXAMPLE]** Offer rate 300 taka → cash refund **150 taka**.

### Money when goods are not taken

| Which money | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Delivery charge** (sent, then came back) | **The whole charge is forfeited** — the cost really happened |
| **Delivery charge** (cancelled before sending) | **50% refunded** — handling cost and time were spent |
| **Product advance** | Goods not taken → **50% refunded** |

### Return analysis

- At month end, each customer's **profile** shows their returns
- In the profile I can see **for what reasons they return most**
- A separate report — **which products are returned most, and why**

### When mistakes are found

- **My staff's mistake** → warning to the staff member
- **The shop's mistake** → warning to them
- If the same reason happens **repeatedly** → **the system sends a warning automatically**: "Your mistake is happening repeatedly"

> **[PROBLEM]** Returned goods came back again.

<!-- -->

> **[SOLUTION]** Those goods carry a **"Previously returned"** mark. If it keeps coming back, the medicine itself is the problem.

<!-- -->

> **[PROBLEM]** The same memo was printed twice and someone tries to return twice.

<!-- -->

> **[SOLUTION]** From the second print, the memo says **"Reprint"**.

<!-- -->

> **[PROBLEM]** One memo has medicine and another product; their rules differ.

<!-- -->

> **[SOLUTION]** They stay on one memo, but return rules differ — the memo states both.
